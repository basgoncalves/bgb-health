# BGB Health — Evidence review: training and nutrition guidelines by client goal

*Internal working document. Version 1, November 2026. Prepared for Basílio Gonçalves and Gwen Bachner.*
*Not for publication. Review once a year or when a major position stand changes.*

---

## 0. How to use this review

Each section gives (1) the current consensus recommendation, (2) the numbers we use as default starting points, and (3) the main source. Defaults are **starting points to individualise**, not prescriptions. Section 9 lists when a client must be referred instead of coached.

**Scope of practice (Austria).** BGB Health advises **healthy adults** on prevention, performance and body composition. Nutrition therapy for diseases (diabetes, kidney disease, eating disorders, food allergies requiring a therapeutic diet, etc.) is reserved for *Diätolog:innen*, and treatment of injuries or pathology for physiotherapists and physicians. Every recommendation below assumes the client has passed pre-participation screening (see the workflow document, step 2).

## 1. Baseline for every client

### 1.1 Physical activity (WHO 2020)
- **Aerobic:** 150–300 min/week moderate, or 75–150 min/week vigorous, or an equivalent mix. More gives additional benefit.
- **Muscle-strengthening:** all major muscle groups on **≥2 days/week**, moderate or higher intensity.
- **Adults ≥65:** add multicomponent balance and strength work ≥3 days/week.
- **Sedentary time:** reduce it and replace with any activity; "some is better than none".

### 1.2 Resistance training (ACSM Position Stand 2026, first update since 2009)
- Any resistance training improves strength, size, power and function; the **largest gain is going from none to some**.
- **Consistency before optimisation.** Train all major muscle groups **at least twice a week**; fine-tune load, volume and frequency later.
- Bands, body weight and home training produce measurable gains — a gym is not required.
- Rigid "ideal" programmes are no longer supported; **goals, enjoyment and adherence** drive programme design, especially for older adults.
- Athletes still need specialised, sport-specific programming.

**Our working defaults** (consistent with the position stand and the wider literature; adjust per client):

| Goal | Sessions / week | Sets per muscle / week | Load | Proximity to failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Health / beginner | 2 | 4–6 | 8–15 reps | stop 2–4 reps short |
| Hypertrophy | 2–3 per muscle | 10–20 | 6–30 reps (load matters little if effort is high) | 0–3 reps short |
| Maximal strength | 2–4 | 6–12 on main lifts | ≥80 % 1RM, 1–6 reps | 1–3 reps short |
| Power / ageing | 2–3 | 3–6 power sets | 30–60 % 1RM, moved fast | well short of failure |

### 1.3 Healthy eating pattern (Austrian recommendations, December 2024)
Austria's updated food pyramid comes in two versions: with fish and meat, and without (ovo-lacto-vegetarian). Legumes are a new category, and the amount of animal products has been reduced, partly for climate reasons.

| Food group | With fish/meat | Without fish/meat |
|---|---|---|
| Fluids | ≥1.5 L/day; ≤4 cups black tea/coffee | same |
| Vegetables & fruit | 5 portions/day (3 veg, 2 fruit); 1 portion = 1 fist | same |
| Grains & potatoes | 4 portions/day (1 = 2 fists; bread = 1 palm) | 5 portions/day |
| Dairy | 1 white + 1 yellow (cheese) per day | 2 white + 1 yellow per day |
| Oils, nuts, seeds | 2 portions/day (1 tbsp oil or 2 tbsp nuts) | same |
| Legumes | ≥3 portions/week (≈125 g cooked) | ≥4 portions/week |
| Fish, meat, eggs | per week: 1 fish, 1 lean meat, +1 fish or meat; ≤3 eggs | ≤4 eggs/week |
| Sweets, salty snacks, sugary drinks | rarely, ≤1 small handful | same |

Clients who don't eat sea fish: 1 tbsp rapeseed oil daily (omega-3).

## 2. Goal: general health and energy for busy working adults

**Typical client:** 30–55, desk job, little time, irregular meals, low energy in the afternoon.

**Training**
- Minimum effective dose: **2 full-body strength sessions of 30–45 min** + daily walking (aim for 7,000–10,000 steps, or 30 min brisk walking).
- Add 1–2 shorter aerobic sessions or intervals if time allows.
- "Exercise snacks" (stairs, 5-min mobility, a walk after lunch) count toward the weekly total.

**Nutrition**
- Base: Austrian plate (section 1.3).
- **Protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**, spread over 3–4 meals of ~0.3–0.4 g/kg.
- Plan a **mid-afternoon protein + fibre snack** to blunt the energy dip; limit sugary drinks.
- Caffeine: keep within ≤4 cups/day and avoid it in the 6–8 h before sleep.

**Measure:** sleep duration, step count, waist circumference, resting heart rate, a simple strength test (e.g. 30-s sit-to-stand, push-ups).

## 3. Goal: women 35+ (perimenopause and postmenopause)

**Why a separate programme:** declining oestrogen accelerates loss of muscle and bone and increases visceral fat; vasomotor symptoms and sleep disruption reduce training capacity.

**Training**
- Follow WHO doses (section 1.1), with **resistance training as non-negotiable** (EMAS 2026 *Menopause Essentials: Exercise*).
- **Bone:** progressive, heavier resistance training plus impact loading. In the LIFTMOR trial (Watson et al., 2018), **supervised high-intensity resistance and impact training** (deadlift, squat, overhead press at ~85 % 1RM, 5×5, plus jumping chin-ups with drop landings), 2×30 min/week for 8 months, **improved bone density in postmenopausal women with low bone mass** with no serious adverse events. Use only with careful technique progression; for clients with diagnosed osteoporosis or a fracture history, coordinate with their physician.
- **Balance and power** work to reduce fall risk.
- **Pelvic-floor training** is part of every programme (EMAS); screen for leakage before heavy lifting or impact.
- Combined aerobic + resistance training may help strength, body fat and inflammation; evidence on vasomotor symptoms is mixed.

**Nutrition**
- **Protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**, ≥25–30 g per meal (older adults need more per meal to stimulate muscle protein synthesis).
- **Calcium** ~1,000 mg/day, mainly from dairy or fortified alternatives; **vitamin D** status checked by the client's doctor (common deficiency in Austrian winters).
- Fibre-rich, legume-heavy pattern (cardiometabolic risk rises after menopause).
- **Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day** can be discussed as an evidence-supported adjunct to resistance training (ISSN 2017); emerging data in women are promising but still limited.

**Measure:** grip strength, sit-to-stand, single-leg balance, jump or power test, waist, symptoms diary (sleep, hot flushes, energy).

## 4. Goal: fat loss / body recomposition

**Training**
- Keep or start **resistance training ≥2×/week** to preserve lean mass in a deficit.
- Physical activity: 150–250 min/week gives modest weight loss; **>250 min/week** is associated with clinically meaningful loss and less regain (ACSM Position Stand, Donnelly et al., 2009).
- Raise daily steps (non-exercise activity) before adding long cardio sessions.

**Nutrition**
- Moderate deficit: target **~0.5–1 % of body weight per week**. Faster loss costs more lean mass and is harder to sustain.
- **Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day**; up to ~2.3–3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass for lean, trained clients in a deficit (Helms et al., 2014).
- High fibre and high-volume, low-energy foods; plan for maintenance after the diet phase.
- **Never** prescribe very low-energy diets; any sign of disordered eating → stop and refer (section 9).

**Measure:** waist, body mass trend (weekly average, not single days), photos if the client wants, strength (should hold or rise).

## 5. Goal: muscle gain and strength

**Training:** section 1.2 hypertrophy/strength rows; progressive overload; 8–12+ weeks before judging results.

**Nutrition**
- **Protein 1.6 g/kg/day** captures most of the benefit for muscle gain; range **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day** (ISSN 2017), up to ~2.2 g/kg is reasonable.
- 20–40 g protein per meal every 3–4 h; a protein-rich meal or snack before sleep can help.
- Small energy surplus (~5–10 % above maintenance) for most; beginners can recompose at maintenance.
- **Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g/day** — the best-supported supplement for strength and lean mass (ISSN 2017).

## 6. Goal: endurance performance (runners, triathletes, cyclists)

**Training**
- Most volume at low intensity, with a smaller share of threshold and high-intensity work.
- **Increase load gradually** and track it (weekly distance or time × session RPE). Big spikes in load relative to the past weeks are associated with injury; there is no single "safe" percentage, so progress conservatively and individually.
- **Strength training 2×/week** improves running economy and resilience; keep it during the season at lower volume.
- Gait and technique analysis (our biomechanics assessment) for clients with recurring overuse pain — but pain or injury → physio/doctor first.

**Nutrition** (ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada joint position, 2016)
- Carbohydrate scaled to load: **3–5 g/kg/day** light training, **5–7** moderate (≈1 h/day), **6–10** high volume, **8–12** extreme.
- During exercise: **30–60 g carbohydrate/h** for 1–2.5 h; up to **90 g/h** (glucose + fructose) for longer events.
- Protein 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day; ~0.3 g/kg after key sessions.
- Hydration: drink to limit body-mass loss to <2 %; replace sodium in long or hot sessions.
- **Caffeine 3–6 mg/kg** ~60 min before can improve performance (ISSN 2021); lower doses (~2–3 mg/kg) often suffice and cause fewer side effects.

## 7. Goal: team and power sports

- Periodise strength, power and speed around the competition calendar; maintain strength in-season (1–2 short sessions/week).
- Injury-prevention programmes (e.g. neuromuscular warm-ups) and hamstring/adductor strengthening.
- Carbohydrate **5–8 g/kg/day** depending on training load; higher around matches.
- Protein 1.6–2.0 g/kg/day; creatine 3–5 g/day can support repeated sprint and strength work.

## 8. Cross-cutting: energy availability and REDs

**Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)** — IOC consensus statement 2023 (Mountjoy et al., *Br J Sports Med* 57:1073–1097):
- Caused by **low energy availability**: energy intake too low for the exercise energy expenditure. Affects women **and men**, recreational and elite.
- Consequences span bone health, menstrual function, metabolism, immunity, mental health, and performance.
- **Low carbohydrate availability** and mental health are now recognised as important contributors.
- Screening and diagnosis use the **IOC REDs Clinical Assessment Tool, version 2 (CAT2)**; diagnosis is a clinical (medical) task.

**What this means for us:** every athlete and every client in a deficit is screened for warning signs (section 9). We do not diagnose REDs; we refer.

## 9. Red flags — refer instead of coaching (or coach only with medical clearance)

| Area | Red flag | Refer to |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | chest pain, unexplained breathlessness, fainting, palpitations, known heart disease | GP / cardiologist before any training |
| Metabolic / disease | diabetes, kidney/liver disease, other diagnosed condition needing a diet | Diätolog:in + physician |
| Eating | signs of disordered eating, very restrictive intake, compensatory exercise | GP; specialised eating-disorder service |
| Energy / REDs | missed or stopped periods (≥3 months), recurrent stress fractures, persistent fatigue, low libido (men) | sports physician |
| Musculoskeletal | acute injury, pain that limits daily life, night pain, numbness | physiotherapist / physician |
| Bone | known osteoporosis or fragility fracture | physician before high-load or impact work |
| Pregnancy / postpartum | pregnancy, early postpartum, pelvic-floor symptoms | gynaecologist / pelvic-floor physio for clearance |
| Mental health | low mood, anxiety affecting daily life, any mention of self-harm | GP; crisis lines if urgent |
| Medication | medicines affecting heart rate, blood sugar or bleeding | GP / pharmacist |

## 10. Supplements we can discuss (healthy adults only)

| Supplement | Evidence | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Creatine monohydrate | strong for strength, lean mass, repeated sprints (ISSN 2017) | 3–5 g/day, no loading needed |
| Caffeine | strong for endurance and many sports (ISSN 2021) | 2–6 mg/kg, 30–60 min before |
| Protein powder | convenience only | when food alone doesn't meet the target |
| Vitamin D | only if deficient (physician tests) | per physician |
| Iron | only if deficient (physician tests) | per physician; never "just in case" |

Advise **batch-tested products** (e.g. Informed Sport / Kölner Liste) for anyone in tested sport.
